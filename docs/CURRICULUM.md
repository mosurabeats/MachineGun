# LINEAGE curriculum design & research notes

## Mission

LINEAGE is a complete tattoo apprenticeship program, not a flash-painting course. It prepares the apprentice for professional tattooing: machine operation, skin preparation, stencils, needle selection, lining, shading, color, client management, sanitation and healing. It combines four modes of learning:

1. **Independent study**: lessons, interactive tools and knowledge checks
2. **Synthetic-skin practice**: assignments with required photo or video evidence
3. **Technical assessment**: AI feedback on visible results, plus mentor-observed milestones
4. **Supervised client tattooing**: only after mentor approval, under an active apprentice license, in a registered shop

## Progression

```
Study + Workshop + Synthetic practice ──► ★ mentor milestones (observed in person)
                                           ├─ BBP training verified
                                           ├─ cross-contamination practice
                                           ├─ full procedure on synthetic skin
                                           ├─ lining competency (synthetic)
                                           ├─ shading/packing competency (synthetic)
                                           └─ mentor readiness decision
                                        + apprentice license recorded, confirmed by mentor
                                        + BBP training current
                                        ──► Supervised client session log unlocks
                                        ──► healing reviews ──► human-skin milestones
                                        ──► hours, portfolio ──► mentor recommendation
                                        ──► licensure application (decided by OPR, not LINEAGE)
```

## Allocation

The default weighting is 30% design, 25% machine and synthetic practice, 20% safety, anatomy and procedure, 15% history and theory, 10% assessment and portfolio. It is adjustable on The Path and always normalised to 100%. When the change is made in Mentor Mode, it is labelled as mentor-set. The intent is to shift toward practical and supervised work over time while keeping up drawing.

## Research findings (Nov 2026)

### Vermont

* **Statute:** 26 V.S.A. chapter 79 (Tattooists and Body Piercers). Tattooists must be licensed or registered with OPR. Apprentices must register with OPR **before** starting the apprenticeship. Shops must register.
* **Rules:** Administrative Rules for Tattooing and Body Piercing, CVR 04-030-310. An apprentice supervisor must have practised actively for at least three years and must have no record of disciplinary action. The copy hosted by the Secretary of State is dated 2004, so confirm which version is current.
* **Hours:** OPR's supervising statement form lists **1,000 hours** for a tattooist (2,000 for a combined tattooist and body piercer). Hours count only while the apprentice license is **active**, and the statement must be completed before the apprenticeship begins.
* **Infection-control training:** a universal precautions / infectious disease course is required. Secondary sources describe a three-hour course with periodic renewal, and some sites cite other figures. **Confirm with OPR.**
* **Age:** tattooing minors is prohibited (secondary sources say this applies even with parental consent). Confirm in the statute.
* **Policy context:** a 2026 testimony on H.588 by a former OPR tattoo advisor argued that oversight is largely reactive. This is an advocacy view.

### Public health

* **OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1030** (Bloodborne Pathogens) applies to occupational exposure to blood and other potentially infectious materials. OSHA has interpretation letters on tattoo establishments.
* **CDC MMWR 2012:** documented tattoo-associated nontuberculous mycobacterial infections linked to contaminated ink and to dilution with non-sterile water. The CDC recommends artists avoid non-sterile dilution.
* **FDA:** regulates tattoo ink as a cosmetic. In 2025 it warned about specific bacteria-contaminated inks. Reactions should be reported (MedWatch) with brand and lot number.

### Professional education (leads to evaluate)

Tattooing 101, Fireside Tattoo Network and Tattoo Smart were named as sources to investigate. They appear in the research library as unverified leads, with notes on how to evaluate them. Their current offerings, instructors and costs were not verified for this release. Manufacturer manuals are treated as the authority on each machine's stroke, voltage, compatibility and maintenance.

### Where educators disagree

The Archive presents each of these with positions, an evidence tag and LINEAGE's approach:

* coil vs rotary vs pen for learning
* practice surfaces
* aftercare regimens
* fixed depth numbers
* voltage recipes
* online-only "apprenticeships"
